# Personas — Zion Kidd Personal Portfolio Site

## 1. The Recruiter/Hiring Manager (Skimmer)
- **Who**: A technical recruiter, hiring manager, or founder evaluating Zion for a role or collaboration.
- **Context**: Has a stack of candidates/portfolios to get through; gives each one 30-60 seconds unless something stands out.
- **Goals**: Quickly answer "is this person worth a deeper look?" — wants signal (real shipped products, real thinking), not a resume restated as a webpage.
- **Frustrations**: Portfolios that bury the point under a wall of screenshots or vague buzzwords; case studies that read like feature lists instead of decisions made.
- **Behavior**: Scans the home page, reads one-line summaries on project cards, opens at most one case study, skims headers rather than reading every paragraph.

## 2. The Technical Peer / Deep-Reader
- **Who**: A fellow developer/designer, potential collaborator, or genuinely curious visitor (e.g. someone who found Vine to Wine and wants to know who built it).
- **Context**: Not evaluating for a hire — evaluating craft and thinking. Has more time and patience than the Skimmer.
- **Goals**: Understand the real problem-solving behind the project — the insight section, the tradeoffs, what was deliberately left out and why.
- **Frustrations**: Surface-level case studies that only show polish, not process; missing technical detail (tech stack, architecture decisions).
- **Behavior**: Reads a case study top to bottom, including the bonus sections; may check the "tech stack" chips and any linked repo.

## 3. Zion (Author/Maintainer)
- **Who**: The site owner, not a visitor — the person who will keep this site updated over time.
- **Context**: Launching with two full case studies and one placeholder ("Coming Soon"), several bio/contact fields intentionally left blank at launch.
- **Goals**: Add the wine society logo rebrand case study later without needing to re-architect anything; fill in bio/contact/resume placeholders post-MVP with minimal edits, not a rebuild.
- **Frustrations**: A site where every new project requires bespoke, one-off page code instead of dropping content into a reusable template.
- **Behavior**: Edits content/data files directly (developer workflow, not a CMS).

## 4. The Direct-Link Visitor
- **Who**: Anyone who arrives at a specific case-study page via a shared link (e.g. a repost on LinkedIn/Twitter, a link in a message) — never saw the home page first.
- **Context**: Has zero prior context about Zion or the rest of the portfolio.
- **Goals**: Quickly orient — whose site is this, what else did they build — without being forced back to the home page first.
- **Frustrations**: Landing on a page with no way back to the rest of the site, or no sense of who the author is, feels disorienting and untrustworthy.
- **Behavior**: Expects visible navigation back to the home page and at least a minimal author identity (name/title) on every case-study page, not just the home page.

## 5. The Product-Curious Visitor
- **Who**: Someone who read a case study and now wants to actually use Vine to Wine or the fitness app themselves — not evaluating Zion as a hire at all.
- **Context**: Arrived via the same case-study pages as the other personas, but with a completely different goal.
- **Goals**: Find a clear, fast path to try or download the actual product.
- **Frustrations**: A case study that only talks about the product without ever linking to it, forcing a separate search.
- **Behavior**: Looks for a distinct "Try it" / "View live" call-to-action, separate from any resume/hiring-oriented CTA on the page.
